Most engineering R&D projects I get called into are stuck between two failure modes. Either the model is elegant but the app around it is a demo, or the app ships but the underlying physics is wrong in ways that only show up under load. Fixing either side in isolation rarely fixes the project.

What actually moves things forward is treating the whole stack as one thing. The Bentley MOSES automation, the FastAPI endpoint that serves the surrogate, the Flutter view that lets a naval architect flip through 15,000 cases, and the DigitalOcean instance that runs the nightly retrain job. Each piece pulls the others into a specific shape. A one-person practice can hold all of that in view.

That is the practice I run. The offshore riser twin, the patent similarity system, the ship recycling expert system, and the fractional CTO work at Openct are the same job in different clothes: pick a problem where the model, the data, and the product all have to agree, then ship the thing.
