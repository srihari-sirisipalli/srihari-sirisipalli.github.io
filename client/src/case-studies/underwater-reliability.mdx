## The question

Underwater propulsion subsystems live in a hostile environment for a long time. Salt water, pressure cycling, marine biofouling, vibration, cavitation. Bearings, shafts, and structural joints all have failure modes that do not look like their above-water cousins. The research question: where does the structure actually fail, how much service life is left, and where are the honest design margins.

## What I work on

**Failure-mode identification.** Which components fail first, under which loading regimes, and how the failure propagates through the subsystem.

**Fatigue prediction.** Load spectra from realistic operating profiles, S-N curves for the joint and material system, and damage accumulation over service life.

**Design margins.** Where the margins are set today, where they are conservative for the wrong reason, and where they are aggressive for the wrong reason.

**Reliability under uncertainty.** Probabilistic assessment of survival given the ranges of loading, material properties, and environmental variables that are actually observed, not the ones assumed on paper.

## AI, DOE, and ML surrogates

The physics simulations that drive reliability estimates are expensive. Running enough of them to sweep the design space, quantify uncertainty, and stress-test margins by brute force is not practical. So the pipeline layers three tools on top of the physics:

**Design of Experiments.** Stratified sampling (Latin Hypercube and space-filling variants) picks the smallest set of physics runs that still covers the design and loading space with useful resolution.

**ML surrogates.** Once the sparse physics runs are in hand, ML surrogates (gradient-boosted trees, MLPs, physics-informed networks depending on the response) approximate the expensive solver at inference cost. That collapses turnaround from hours per case to milliseconds.

**AI-assisted exploration.** With a fast surrogate in place, sensitivity analysis, sampling-based reliability estimation, and inverse design (find configurations that meet a target reliability at minimum weight, say) all become tractable. AI here is a tool for exploring a design space at scale, not a replacement for the physics.
