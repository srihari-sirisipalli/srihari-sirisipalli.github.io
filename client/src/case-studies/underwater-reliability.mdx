## The question

Underwater propulsion subsystems live in a hostile environment for a long time. Salt water, pressure cycling, marine biofouling, vibration, cavitation. Bearings, shafts, and structural joints all have failure modes that don't look like their above-water cousins. The research question: where does the structure actually fail, how much service life is left, and where are the honest design margins?

## What I work on

**Failure-mode identification.** Which components fail first, under which loading regimes, and how the failure propagates through the subsystem.

**Fatigue prediction.** Load spectra from realistic operating profiles, S-N curves for the joint and material system, and damage accumulation over service life.

**Design margins.** Where the margins are set today, where they're conservative for the wrong reason, and where they're aggressive for the wrong reason.

**Reliability under uncertainty.** Probabilistic assessment of survival given the ranges of loading, material properties, and environmental variables that are actually observed, not the ones assumed on paper.

## The framing

Ongoing research inside the broader underwater programme. Specific components, clients, and results stay with the parties who own them. What generalises is the method: how to reason about reliability when the environment is hostile and the observation window is short.
