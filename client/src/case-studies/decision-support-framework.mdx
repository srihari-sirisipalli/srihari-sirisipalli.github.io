## The question

A digital technology sits in front of an industrial team. Should they adopt it? A vendor demo is not an answer. Neither is a spreadsheet of features. The real question is multi-dimensional: does this technology fit the technical environment, is the organisation ready to operate it, what breaks if it fails, and does it move the needle far enough on the outcome that actually matters?

This is a research project on the shape of that decision.

## What I'm exploring

**Technical fit.** How the technology sits inside the existing stack, data pipeline, integration surface, and skill base. Not just "does it work" but "does it work with what we already have without a two-year migration."

**Operational readiness.** Whether the team can run it, maintain it, and evolve it after the vendor's implementation window closes. Ownership after go-live is usually where adoption succeeds or fails.

**Risk surface.** What breaks if the technology underperforms, is deprecated, or the vendor goes away. Cost of exit, lock-in, and the honest question of what happens on a bad day.

**Impact.** Does the change actually move the outcome that mattered? Efficiency, safety, revenue, capability, insight. If the answer to this is "we're not sure," everything upstream is noise.

**Decision surface.** Bringing these dimensions together into a structure that a real decision-making group can use in a room, in an afternoon, without a data science team.

## Where this comes from

I've spent time inside enough industrial R&D and operational environments to see the same pattern play out: a promising technology, an enthusiastic pilot, and a stalled adoption a year later. The bottleneck is rarely the technology itself. It's the frame used to decide.

## What's in it

A working set of criteria and weightings. Scoring rubrics. Case notes from technologies I've watched succeed and fail in real environments. Comparative studies. A worked example or two. The output is a framework, not a product.
