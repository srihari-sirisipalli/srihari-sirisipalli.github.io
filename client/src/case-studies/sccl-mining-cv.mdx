## What it is

An end-to-end computer vision engagement for SCCL (Singareni Collieries) mining operations. It has two halves: a documentation deliverable that anchors the strategy and architecture, and a runnable software demonstrator that shows what the operator experience feels like.

## The documentation half

Three polished deliverable PDFs form the outward artefacts of the engagement:

- **Master Plan.** The strategy document. 8 parts covering business case, phased rollout, and operating model.
- **Technical Document.** The engineering reference. 12 parts covering system architecture, integrations, data flow, and each analytics feature end-to-end.
- **GUI Design Document.** The UX and UI reference. 10 parts covering wireframes, interaction patterns, and the operator dashboard's information architecture.

Behind those PDFs sits a deep-research corpus: one synthesis document plus nine numbered dimensions covering industry context, operational readiness, technology choices, and risk. Every architectural claim in the deliverables traces back to that research.

## The software half

**Analytics backend.** A Python package covering eleven video analytics features: PPE detection, automatic number-plate recognition (ANPR), vehicle counting and headcount, perimeter monitoring (Sentinel), change detection, dust-plume detection, conveyor flow, stockpile volume estimation, thermal anomaly detection, and drone GPS playback.

**NOC dashboard.** A Dash-based Network Operations Centre view. Estate map, per-site view, drone HUD, camera mosaic, camera detail, shift log, and audit trail, all wired to the analytics backend via simulator or inference loops depending on the demo mode.

**Design constraint.** The software is built strictly from what the documentation commits to. If it isn't in the deliverables, it isn't in the demonstrator. That constraint keeps the two halves honest.

## Why the split

Mining operations don't adopt technology on a demo alone. Procurement and safety teams need a document trail; operators need to feel the interface. The engagement delivers both from a single canonical source, which turns "does this actually work" into a live click-through, not a promise.
